night + curriculum: SGDR ineffective, NS optimisations both slower, curriculum ready

Four measurements ran unattended overnight and three were negative, which is the point of
recording them.

SGDR (--lr-restarts, the user's suggestion): two arms of 1500 steps, validation loss every
250. Arm B with two cycles was worse than arm A at every single checkpoint, including after
the restart at step 781 (+0.1412 there). The implementation defaults to bit-identical
behaviour, so nothing existing changes.

Batched NS: grouping same-shape matrices and using torch.bmm gave 2.677 s against 2.386 s
for the per-matrix loop, i.e. 0.89x. Results correct to 3.5e-06, but bmm is not faster than
mm on this CPU.

Async NS: the user pointed out that the previous test was not actually asynchronous --
ns_device() finished the queue per matrix, so it was alternation. Rewritten to enqueue every
matrix before any CPU work. iGPU alone 1.788 s, CPU alone 1.151 s, concurrent 3.454 s
against a serial sum of 3.278 s, so 5% worse than serial and 0.52x of ideal. And the first
reason alone is enough: the iGPU is 1.55x slower at NS than the CPU, because NS is 512x512
and 512x256 shapes and the iGPU is poor at small shapes.

TK sweep: throughput falls as TK grows, 167.7 at TK=4 down to 20.7 at TK=32. That is the
opposite of what staging overhead predicts and consistent with the LDS measurement.

Also: the 300 GFLOPS baseline in 7.22.4 is not reproducible. igpu_gemm_v4.py now yields
126.9 and produces incorrect results. The absolute figures from that kernel are withdrawn;
the relative comparisons in this work stand on their own measurements.

Curriculum infrastructure, which is what remains: token_scale (one token occupying an sxs
block of the coordinate grid, so a 16x16 image reuses a grid-32 position table) verified
bit-identical at scale 1 and correct at 2 and 4; --total_tokens override so both phases
build the same model; the 16x16 token file from all 14432 images. The A/B is queued: warm up
800 steps at 16x16, then 1500 at 32x32, judged on wall-clock time to a given validation loss
per 7.27.4.
