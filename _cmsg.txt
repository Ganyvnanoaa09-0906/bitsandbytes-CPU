feat(quant): 2-bit weights, format chosen by measurement and verified against an independent result

The user asked for quantisation more aggressive than NF4. What this establishes:

  * NF4 is not optimal at 4 bits. On real weights, blockwise with absmax 64, uniform
    beats it by 8.3% on relative L2 error, and NF's advantage only appears as width
    shrinks -- 1.2% ahead at 2 bits. With 16 levels the quantile spacing wastes
    resolution; with 4 it matters where the levels sit.
  * Downstream loss on the trained 32x32 AR checkpoint: fp32 5.5038, 4 bit +0.004,
    3 bit +0.019, 2 bit +0.117 (nf) / +0.132 (uniform). So 3 bits is essentially
    free and 2 bits costs about 2%.
  * On the real loss NF and uniform are indistinguishable at 4 and 3 bits and NF
    only wins at 2 -- agreeing with the proxy study in direction, and confirming
    again that the proxy and the criterion give different answers.
  * A packed implementation (4 indices per byte, bit order documented because
    getting it wrong is silent) with Linear2bit and Embedding2bit. Four
    configurations reproduce independently measured losses to within 0.001:
    5.5038/5.5038, 5.5538/5.5538, 5.6198/5.6206, 5.6352/5.6354.

Also measured: quantising embeddings costs 0.066 of validation loss against 0.050 for
the Linear layers, while being 2 of 63 tensors -- so embeddings are better left at
fp32, which is a decision with a number behind it rather than an assumption.

One test in this series reported a mismatch that was not a bug: convert() rewrites
only nn.Linear while the study quantised every ndim>=2 tensor, so two arms quantised
identical sets and produced bit-identical losses. The comparison was empty. Same
failure as elsewhere in this work -- a check that cannot separate the cases it exists
to separate.

Combined with the 4-bit optimiser state (1.03 bytes/param), the training footprint
becomes 1.34 bytes/param against 12.0 for fp32, which is what brings a 3B model to
4.0 GB and therefore inside this machine's 15.4 GB.
